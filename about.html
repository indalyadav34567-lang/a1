HERITAGE DISCOVERY

The 1824 Molecular Breakthrough

In 1824, Scottish chemist Charles Macintosh discovered that by dissolving natural Para rubber in coal-tar naphtha, he could apply a thin, flexible elastomer solution between two plies of woven cotton gabardine. The result was the world’s first completely impervious, waterproof textile that did not turn brittle in arctic freezing or melt in summer warmth.

Today, Macintoshcoat upholds this unbroken Scottish tradition. While commercial brands migrated to cheap polyurethane petroleum coatings that decay and crack in a few seasons, our vulcanized bonded cotton remains chemically stable for over three decades of severe downpours.
